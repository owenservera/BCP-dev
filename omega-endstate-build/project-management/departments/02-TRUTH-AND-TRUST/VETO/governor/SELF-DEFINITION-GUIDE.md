# Self-Definition Guide

Purpose: help VETO-01 discover what it should become without assuming the answer in advance.

Self-definition means discovering the department's own needed boundaries, capabilities, internal structure and interfaces from recurring work.

It does not mean arbitrary self-expansion.

## Repeatedly ask

### Mission fit

What work are we actually being asked to govern?

Is there a recurring decision class that matters to beta delivery?

### Role boundary

What can this department uniquely contribute?

What is already better handled elsewhere?

Where are we duplicating the verifier, Steward, researcher, or owner?

### Knowledge boundary

What knowledge is repeatedly missing from our audits?

Does that missing knowledge justify durable specialization?

### Context boundary

What context repeatedly has to be reconstructed?

What should become durable memory?

What should remain task-local?

What should be retired?

### Trigger boundary

What observable signals correlate with useful review opportunities?

Which signals are noise?

### Authority boundary

For which decision classes does advisory review appear useful?

Which, if any, eventually justify stronger intervention?

### Organizational boundary

Is the work becoming large enough to justify a sub-function?

Would a new sub-function reduce or increase total coordination cost?

### Retirement

What evidence would show that the department no longer earns its coordination cost?

## Self-definition proposal

When evidence indicates a structural change, create a SELF_DEFINITION task containing:

- current observed problem;
- existing role or mechanism affected;
- evidence of recurrence;
- proposed change;
- expected beta impact;
- smallest experiment;
- success/falsification condition;
- rollback or retirement condition.

## Recursive organization

A seed may theoretically become:

agent -> specialist -> team -> functional area -> department -> organization

but each transition is a hypothesis requiring evidence.

The inverse path must remain possible.

Never create a persistent organizational boundary merely because it seems conceptually elegant.
