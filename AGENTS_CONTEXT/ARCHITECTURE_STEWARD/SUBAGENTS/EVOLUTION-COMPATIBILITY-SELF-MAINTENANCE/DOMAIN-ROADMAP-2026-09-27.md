## Strategic Objective

Make VIVIM able to **change without becoming semantically untrustworthy**.

A successful CFA-09 destination is not a universal “evolution engine.” It is a coherent temporal/change discipline that lets VIVIM:

1. identify exactly what is changing and what semantic delta that represents;
2. derive the known impact before a consequential change;
3. evaluate compatibility on the dimensions that actually matter;
4. preserve canonical identity, relationships, Work, evidence, Product Instance continuity and user ownership;
5. apply bounded migrations, replacements, repairs and upgrades safely;
6. distinguish candidate, tested, verified, compatible, authorized, promoted and active states;
7. quarantine, roll back, reconstruct or retire a change without erasing history;
8. automate only changes whose semantics, impact, authority, reversibility and verification are sufficiently bounded;
9. make evolution itself observable and explainable;
10. stop safely when the system does not know enough.

The enduring test is:

> **Can VIVIM evolve across a real boundary while the person’s world remains the same owned, attributable, recoverable world unless the change explicitly means otherwise?**

---

## Responsibility Frontier

CFA-09 owns the **cross-domain governance and semantic continuity of consequential change**.

It does **not** own the canonical semantics of the thing being changed.

The working loop is:

```
CHANGE SIGNAL
→ IDENTIFY SUBJECT + SEMANTIC DELTA
→ RECOVER CURRENT/HISTORICAL STATE
→ DERIVE IMPACT
→ CHECK MATERIAL COMPATIBILITY
→ RECHECK AUTHORITY IMPLICATIONS
→ APPLY BOUNDED CHANGE
→ VERIFY
→ PROMOTE / ACTIVATE OR QUARANTINE
→ MONITOR CONTINUITY
→ ROLLBACK / RECOVER / RETIRE
```

The roadmap therefore treats:

- Data migration as a change/continuity problem, not a second storage system.
- Provider healing as a domain-specific repair mechanism whose broader consequences are governed here.
- Forge as a composition/candidate-generation mechanism whose cross-version continuity is governed here.
- Work as the execution owner whose active contracts must survive, stop, or migrate explicitly when the basis changes.
- Authority as the sole authority owner; compatibility never becomes authorization.
- Runtime as the owner of non-bypassable admission/fencing/activation.
- Experience as the owner of presentation/re-entry; CFA-09 owns the staleness/continuity requirement generated by change.
- Evidence as a cross-cutting requirement carried through every consequential change.

### Boundary rule

A peer request in this roadmap is **not automatically a dependency**. It becomes a confirmed dependency only when the consuming decision is explicit, the supplying CFA owns the subject, and the evidence requirement is confirmed during later reconciliation.

# Conceptual Roadmap

## M1 — Make Change a First-Class Semantic Unit

### Conceptual outcome

VIVIM can unambiguously represent **what changed, from what state, toward what proposed state, why, under which change class, and what lifecycle state the change occupies**, without making the record itself a new authority store.

The minimum reusable object is the **change/evolution record plus its state transition vocabulary**, not an “evolution engine.”

### Why it matters

Without a stable semantic change unit, every later migration, compatibility check, rollback or repair invents its own vocabulary. That creates drift exactly where continuity needs one shared language.

### Evidence basis

- Evolution canonical model defines `changeId`, subject/state references, reason, class, impact, compatibility, authority, evidence, promotion and rollback references.
- E-01–E-14 already distinguish representation, meaning, history, candidate status, compatibility, authority, impact, automation and rollback.
- Destination responsibility R-115/R-116/R-117 makes Evolution/Compatibility/Impact separate first-class responsibilities.

### Current maturity

**Design: L1-ish / partial conceptual coherence.**
**Implementation: fragmented ingredients exist; no single reconciled contract.**

### Design choices at this stage

1. Minimum fields and reference semantics of the change record.
2. Change classes: maintenance vs governed evolution vs constitutional evolution.
3. State machine and terminal/branching semantics.
4. Semantic delta declaration rules.
5. Distinction between version identity and trust/lifecycle state.
6. Which fields are authored evidence versus derived projections.
7. What must remain historically addressable.
8. Whether multiple subjects can be governed in one change transaction and how atomicity is represented.

### Success criteria

**Design validity**
- A reviewer can describe the semantic difference between maintenance, governed evolution and constitutional amendment without relying on implementation details.
- A change record can name its subject and semantic delta without embedding another CFA’s canonical semantics.
- Candidate/verified/compatible/authorized/promoted/active are not collapsed.

**Implementation validity**
- A pure validator/state-transition model can reject invalid lifecycle transitions using deterministic fixtures.
- Change records reference existing canonical identities rather than creating a parallel identity store.

**Integration validity**
- At least two existing domain seams (for example Provider/Realization and Work, or Data and Product Instance) can point to the same change vocabulary without custom reinterpretation.

**Live/external proof**
- Not required for M1; the record semantics must instead be ready to carry later live evidence.

**Product proof**
- A user-facing explanation could answer “what changed and what is active now?” from the same change record without inventing new semantics.

### Falsifiers

- Two materially different change types require incompatible record vocabularies.
- A change cannot be represented without copying another CFA’s canonical store.
- A valid lifecycle requires silently overwriting prior history.
- “Verified” and “Compatible” cannot be separated deterministically.

### Prerequisites / dependencies

**Required now:** existing Ω law and Evolution canonical-model evidence.

**Target peer confirmation:** CFA-01/03 for semantic-delta references; CFA-02 for canonical identity/revision references; CFA-04 for authority-result reference semantics; CFA-10 for any enforcement-state references.

These are **HIGH-VALUE confirmation gates**, not blockers to the initial local design pass.

## M2 — Derive Impact and Compatibility Before Change

### Conceptual outcome

VIVIM can take a proposed change and produce a **material, inspectable impact set plus a dimensioned compatibility result**, explicitly retaining UNKNOWN/CONFLICTED outcomes.

### Why it matters

The dangerous failure mode is not only a bad change; it is a change that appears safe because the system failed to discover what it affects.

### Evidence basis

- E-06 establishes multidimensional compatibility.
- E-07 establishes impact-before-change.
- Destination R-116/R-117 make Compatibility and Impact first-class.
- Existing dependency graph work and change-impact/revalidation evidence give a starting substrate.

### Design choices

1. Which compatibility dimensions are mandatory, optional or inapplicable per change.
2. How compatibility evidence is aggregated without turning the result into a generic score.
3. How impact is derived from canonical references, relationships and active Work.
4. How UNKNOWN impact blocks, quarantines or routes a change.
5. How transitive impact is bounded.
6. How stale/conflicted evidence affects compatibility.
7. How causal/evidence links are represented.
8. Which graph tooling belongs in Architecture Steward vs CFA-09.

### Success criteria

**Design validity**
- A change declares only the compatibility dimensions relevant to the actual subject and effect.
- “Compatible” cannot be emitted without dimension-level evidence.
- Unknown impact is represented as a first-class unresolved condition.

**Implementation validity**
- A deterministic evaluator can compute an impact/compatibility report for a bounded fixture.
- The evaluator distinguishes zero impact from unknown impact.

**Integration validity**
- Provider/Work/Data fixture scenarios demonstrate that one change can reach multiple dependent domains without becoming their owner.

**Live/external proof**
- For a live provider change, observed behavior can be attached to a compatibility dimension rather than being treated as a generic “health” claim.

**Product proof**
- The person can be shown what will be affected, what is known, and what remains unknown before a consequential change.

### Falsifiers

- A compatibility decision can be made without naming the dimensions it depends on.
- Impact derivation returns an empty set when graph knowledge is incomplete.
- A single API/schema test is sufficient for a semantic change.
- The model cannot preserve contradictory evidence without flattening it.

### Dependencies

**Required:** M1 semantics.

**Target peer confirmations:** CFA-01/03, CFA-02, CFA-04, CFA-05, CFA-06, CFA-07, CFA-08, CFA-10.

Actual dependencies are expected to be change-slice-specific and will be confirmed by evidence.

## M3 — Preserve Identity, Relationship, Work and Product Continuity

### Conceptual outcome

VIVIM can evolve data, identity, relationships and durable Work without silently turning continuity into equivalence.

The system can state whether it is:

- the same canonical object with a new revision;
- a new object related to an old one;
- an external identity newly corresponded to a local one;
- or a reconciliation of prior objects into one semantic identity.

### Why it matters

A schema migration that preserves bytes can still destroy meaning. A replacement that preserves behavior can still break historical attribution. Continuity is where semantic trust becomes durable.

### Evidence basis

- Evolution canonical model explicitly distinguishes identity correspondence and relationship evolution.
- D-333 establishes Ω as the sole migration substrate and preserves legacy as evidence only.
- Existing vault revisions and migration evidence provide material for bounded experiments.
- Product Vision makes Product Instance continuity a destination requirement.

### Design choices

1. Identity correspondence record shape.
2. Merge/split/alias semantics and uncertainty.
3. Relationship supersession and contradiction preservation.
4. Effective-time vs revision-time semantics for changed facts.
5. Active Work version pinning and migration/stop rules.
6. Product Instance continuity across install/update/reopen.
7. Reconstruction semantics after partial or failed migration.
8. Which transformations may be automatic versus proposal-gated.

### Success criteria

**Design validity**
- Identity correspondence is never inferred solely from matching fields.
- Relationship provenance, temporal context and contradiction can survive a migration.
- Work identity/history and Product Instance identity are distinct from implementation version identity.

**Implementation validity**
- A bounded round-trip fixture can transform, reconstruct and compare history without deleting the prior interpretation.
- Identity merge/split fixtures preserve correspondence evidence.

**Integration validity**
- At least one scenario crosses Data → Work and one crosses Data → Product Instance continuity.

**Live/external proof**
- A real or high-fidelity external import/reconstruction can be re-opened with prior evidence still addressable.

**Product proof**
- The person sees the same owned world after a compatible change, while meaningful identity changes are explicitly visible.

### Falsifiers

- A semantic migration cannot preserve the old interpretation.
- A relationship merge creates provenance that never existed.
- Active Work loses the version/contract it was authorized against.
- Restore requires rewriting canonical history rather than reconstructing disposable state.

### Dependencies

**Required:** M1; M2 for impact/compatibility on consequential migrations.

**Hard dependency by slice:** CFA-02 canonical persistence/revision semantics; CFA-05 Work contract for active Work migration; CFA-04 when migration changes authority-relevant meaning.

## M4 — Govern Replacement, Promotion, Quarantine and Rollback Across the Plugin Ecosystem

### Conceptual outcome

Everything-is-a-Plugin gains a single evolution-facing lifecycle discipline: a plugin, capability realization, provider realization or composition can be proposed/replaced/tested/promoted/quarantined/rolled back without turning promotion into authority or erasing the predecessor.

### Why it matters

The extensibility model is only useful if replacement is safe. This is the bridge between plugin composability and continuity.

### Evidence basis

- Destination explicitly treats plugin boundaries as the extensibility/replacement seam under Evolution.
- D-315 supplies version-pinned quarantine/rollback evidence.
- D-326 supplies evidence-bearing provider-healing transitions.
- D-418 fixes the shipping browser realization posture for the first live product path.

### Design choices

1. Cross-version compatibility contract for capabilities/realizations/compositions.
2. Promotion prerequisites vs Authority approval vs Runtime activation.
3. Quarantine semantics and preserved historical addressability.
4. Rollback semantics for stateful and stateless replacements.
5. Composition compatibility when one member changes.
6. Provider-healing-to-generic-evolution handoff.
7. Active Work behavior during replacement.
8. Retirement semantics and tombstone/historical reference policy.

### Success criteria

**Design validity**
- Candidate, tested, verified, compatible, authorized, promoted and active remain separate.
- Promotion mechanics cannot become semantic authority.
- Provider healing and Forge generate/repair candidates without bypassing the ordinary governed path.

**Implementation validity**
- Existing version-pinning/quarantine primitives can be exercised through a common change lifecycle fixture.
- Replacement/rollback tests preserve the predecessor and intervening evidence.

**Integration validity**
- One plugin/composition replacement and one provider-realization repair can use the same cross-domain change lifecycle without requiring duplicate lifecycle definitions.

**Live/external proof**
- The real provider/browser realization can be replaced or quarantined in a controlled live test when a supported experiment warrants it.

**Product proof**
- A user can understand why a replacement is active, quarantined, or rolled back and what remains preserved.

### Falsifiers

- A Forge-generated candidate receives privilege that a hand-authored plugin cannot receive.
- Provider healing can promote an unverified realization.
- Rollback deletes the failed version’s evidence.
- “Promoted” automatically means “authorized.”
- Two replacement paths require semantically incompatible lifecycle rules.

### Dependencies

**Required:** M1–M3.

**Target peer confirmations:** CFA-06 and CFA-07 are especially important; CFA-04 and CFA-10 remain independent authority/enforcement owners.

| Forge proving/assembly | ALREADY EXISTS / EXTEND | Candidate generation, never authority |
| Cross-CFA lifecycle conformance suite | NEEDS SMALL EXTENSION | Detect divergent replacement semantics |
| New universal plugin lifecycle engine | NOT YET NEEDED | First prove shared semantics around existing mechanisms |

## M5 — Bound Self-Maintenance, Recovery and Evolution Economics

### Conceptual outcome

VIVIM can maintain itself automatically **only within a declared safety envelope**, while out-of-envelope cases stop at proposal, refusal, quarantine or human/owner gate.

The model explicitly includes the resources consumed by maintenance/evolution without turning economics into an independent global authority.

### Why it matters

Self-maintenance is valuable only if it remains predictable. “Automatic” must mean bounded, not privileged.

### Design choices

1. Safe-envelope predicates.
2. Required evidence/authority/reversibility conditions.
3. Automatic vs proposed vs blocked transitions.
4. Recovery levels: rebuild disposable state, retry/reconcile, restore, rollback, quarantine, retire.
5. Failure escalation and owner attention triggers.
6. Resource reservation and fairness signals for maintenance.
7. Protection of foreground Work and user responsiveness.
8. Constitutional-change stop conditions.

### Success criteria

**Design validity**
- The boundary between deterministic maintenance, governed evolution and constitutional evolution is mechanically describable.
- Safe automation cannot self-expand its own authority.
- Resource pressure can delay or stop maintenance without changing semantic ownership.

**Implementation validity**
- A bounded maintenance fixture automatically completes only when all safety predicates hold.
- An out-of-envelope fixture refuses/stops/quarantines rather than improvising.

**Integration validity**
- Maintenance can coexist with Work, Authority and Runtime rules without bypassing any of them.

**Live/external proof**
- A supported external/resource failure can trigger a bounded maintenance or quarantine path and leave evidence of the decision.

**Product proof**
- The user can tell what VIVIM fixed automatically, what it refused to do, and what needs attention.

### Falsifiers

- The automation path can change its own safety envelope.
- Unknown impact is treated as zero for convenience.
- A maintenance task widens authority or ownership.
- Recovery requires mutating canonical truth to repair a derived projection.
- Resource exhaustion causes silent loss of user-owned continuity.

### Dependencies

**Required:** M1–M4.

Resource economics becomes a **target dependency only where an actual maintenance workload competes materially for scarce resources**.

| Full maintenance scheduler | NOT YET NEEDED | Work/Scheduler ownership remains CFA-05 |
| Autonomous “self-modification” service | NOT ALLOWED | Wrong abstraction and authority boundary |

## M6 — Prove Evolution on a Real Product / External-World Path

### Conceptual outcome

The change discipline is proven not only on documents and fixtures but on a real VIVIM product evolution path, ideally crossing Product Instance continuity plus the Chrome-first provider realization.

The target proof is not “Evolution is finished.” It is:

> **one consequential, end-to-end change can be proposed, impacted, compatibility-checked, authorized where required, applied, verified, activated/quarantined, explained, and recovered while preserving the user’s world and evidence.**

### Why it matters

Evolution is a cross-cutting concern. Its credibility comes from surviving a real composed journey, not from a beautiful standalone model.

### Candidate proof slices

1. Product Instance close/reopen continuity across an update or migration.
2. Provider/browser realization replacement with preserved Work/history.
3. User-created composition change with promotion/quarantine.
4. Known provider drift → evidence → bounded repair → verification → promotion.
5. Derived projection rebuild after canonical change without canonical mutation.

### Design choices

1. Which first live slice gives the strongest evidence at lowest irreversible risk.
2. Exact end-to-end evidence package.
3. Which states are live vs fixture-substituted.
4. How product proof differs from architecture proof.
5. What operational telemetry is necessary without creating prohibited centralization.
6. Exit criteria for moving from experiment to normal product lifecycle.

### Success criteria

**Design validity**
- The chosen live slice maps every phase of the common change lifecycle to named owners and evidence.
- Remaining UNKNOWN/CONFLICTED items are explicit.

**Implementation validity**
- The bounded slice executes through existing governed substrate rather than a parallel test-only architecture.

**Integration validity**
- At minimum, change + Data/continuity + Authority + Work + Runtime are evidenced together; Provider/Forge/Surface are included where the slice genuinely exercises them.

**Live/external proof**
- Real external behavior is captured where the chosen slice requires it, with exact realization/version evidence.
- A failure or quarantine path is exercised or a high-fidelity falsifier proves that the stop condition works.

**Product proof**
- A person can update/reopen/inspect the evolved environment and retain understandable continuity.
- The product can explain what changed, what remains active, what was preserved, and what needs user action.

### Falsifiers

- A real update requires silently rebuilding user identity.
- Live replacement succeeds but evidence cannot name the actual realization/version.
- The system cannot recover without erasing intervening history.
- The end-to-end path requires an undocumented privilege outside the ordinary capability/authority/runtime path.
- Product-level continuity diverges from canonical continuity.

### Dependencies

M6 consumes **confirmed** dependencies discovered through M1–M5 and the later central roadmap. It should not be scheduled solely from this first-pass roadmap.

### Tooling / substrate

| Need | Status | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Existing Chrome/provider lab | ALREADY EXISTS / EXTEND | Live external proof |
| Existing Ω gate/test/falsifier infrastructure | ALREADY EXISTS | Regression and invariant proof |

# Product / Strategic Consequences

A coherent CFA-09 capability changes VIVIM from a system that can **have versions** into one that can **remain the same owned environment while versions change**.

Strategic effects:

- Enables safe evolution of first-party and user-created compositions rather than treating updates as opaque reinstalls.
- Makes the Everything-is-a-Plugin architecture credible as a replacement architecture, not just a composition vocabulary.
- Protects Product Instance identity across close/reopen/update/recovery paths.
- Prevents provider healing and Forge from becoming covert privileged mutation channels.
- Gives Work a durable relationship to the exact capability/realization version it used.
- Makes external change a first-class event with evidence rather than a mysterious breakage.
- Creates the semantic foundation for Self-Knowledge to explain what changed, what is active and what requires the user.
- Supports sovereignty by preserving user ownership and export/reconstruction continuity through change.

The product must not expose every internal lifecycle state indiscriminately. The architecture should preserve rich evidence while surfaces provide a comprehensible, deterministic explanation.
